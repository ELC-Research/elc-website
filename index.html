Reimagining language competence as ecological, embodied action

The ELC Assessment Research Team brings together distinguished scholars from across the globe to advance ecological approaches to language teaching, learning, and assessment.

We bridge traditional assessment frameworks with real-world scenarios, developing innovative tools and theories that recognise languaging as embodied, distributed, and adaptive coordination with people, artefacts, and environments.

Our mission

Three pillars of our work

Our research is grounded in ecological theory, methodological innovation, and a commitment to educational impact across diverse contexts.

🌿

Theoretical re-framing

Moving beyond context-abstracted, code-bound notions of competence toward an ecological understanding of languaging as embodied, situation-sensitive action coordinated with others, artefacts, and environments.

🔧

Assessment innovation

Developing Bayesian-informed approaches that track learner development by integrating prior accomplishment, expert judgement, and emerging performance into probabilistic trajectories of growth.

🌍

Global collaboration

Building sustainable partnerships across institutions and continents to translate ecological languaging research into classroom practice, teacher education, and assessment policy.

Research projects

Current investigations

Our collaborative projects span theoretical development, assessment design, classroom implementation, AI-for-Education, and teacher professional learning across diverse educational contexts.

🌿

ELC Theoretical Framework Development

Principal Investigators: Paul J. Thibault, Constant Leung & Angel M. Y. Lin

Refining the conceptual architecture of ecological languaging competencies, articulating the relationships between embodied action, distributed cognition, and situational coordination in communicative practice.

Theoretical Conceptual framing Distributed language
📊

Bayesian Trajectory Assessment System

Principal Investigators: Constant Leung, Paul J. Thibault, Eunice Jang, Minnie Man Zhu, Angel M. Y. Lin & Ming Ming Chiu

Designing and validating assessment instruments that use Bayesian principles to track learner development, integrating prior accomplishment, expert judgement, and emerging performance data.

Assessment Bayesian methods Learning analytics
🎓

Teacher Professional Development for ELC

Principal Investigators: Constant Leung & Yiqi Liu

Developing professional learning programmes that support teachers in reconceptualising communicative competence and implementing ecological languaging principles in curriculum and materials design.

Teacher education Curriculum design Professional learning
🤖

AI-Mediated Feedback and Languaging

Principal Investigators: Qinghua Chen & Tracy Lu Xi

Investigating how generative AI can be harnessed to provide ecological, formative feedback on student languaging performances while preserving the embodied and distributed nature of communication.

Artificial intelligence Formative assessment Feedback design
🌍

Ecological Languaging and Learning as Wayfinding

Principal Investigators: Paul J. Thibault, Li Wei & Michelle Mingyue Gu

Examining how multilingual learners navigate complex communicative ecologies, drawing on translanguaging practices and digital trans-literacies to make meaning across boundaries.

Multilingualism Translanguaging Digital literacies
🔧

4T Lenses for Educational Material Design

Principal Investigators: Mahtab Janfada & Angel M. Y. Lin

Operationalising the temporal, translingual, transmodal, and transformative lenses for designing classroom tasks and materials that elicit and develop ecological languaging competencies.

Task design Materials development 4T framework
